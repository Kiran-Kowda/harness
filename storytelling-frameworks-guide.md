## 1. STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)

**Structure:**
- **Situation**: Set the context
- **Task**: Define the challenge/goal
- **Action**: Explain what you did
- **Result**: Show the outcome with metrics

**Best for:**
- Job interviews (behavioral questions)
- Performance reviews
- Case studies
- Team retrospectives
- Portfolio presentations

**Why it works:** Linear, logical, evidence-based. Low cognitive load. Emphasizes accountability and outcomes.

**Example:**
> **S:** "Our conversion rate was 2.1%, 40% below industry average."
> **T:** "Needed to get to 3.5% without increasing ad spend."
> **A:** "Ran 12 A/B tests on CTAs, simplified form from 9 to 3 fields, added social proof."
> **R:** "Hit 3.8% in 6 weeks, generated $2.3M additional pipeline."

**When to avoid:** When you need emotional connection or vision casting. STAR is analytical, not inspirational.

## 2. Hero's Journey (Joseph Campbell)

**Structure:**
- **Ordinary World**: Hero in normal life
- **Call to Adventure**: Problem/opportunity appears
- **Refusal of Call**: Resistance/doubt
- **Meeting the Mentor**: Guidance received
- **Crossing Threshold**: Commitment to change
- **Tests, Allies, Enemies**: Challenges faced
- **Ordeal**: The big crisis
- **Reward**: Success achieved
- **Return**: Bringing wisdom back

**Best for:**
- Customer success stories
- Brand origin stories
- Founder narratives
- Cultural transformation stories
- Long-form content marketing

**Why it works:** Primal narrative structure humans recognize unconsciously. Creates emotional identification. Works across cultures.

**Example:**
> "Sarah was drowning in spreadsheets [Ordinary]. Her CEO demanded real-time analytics [Call]. She thought 'We can't afford enterprise tools' [Refusal]. Then she discovered Atlan [Mentor]. She piloted it with one team [Threshold]. Faced IT resistance, won over skeptics [Tests]. Almost lost the project during a security review [Ordeal]. But deployed company-wide [Reward]. Now she's evangelizing data democratization across her industry [Return]."

**When to avoid:** Time-constrained presentations. Board meetings. Anything analytical or dry.

## 3. PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve)

**Structure:**
- **Problem**: State the pain point
- **Agitate**: Make it visceral, urgent
- **Solve**: Present your solution

**Best for:**
- Sales emails
- Ad copy
- Cold outreach
- Product launches
- Pain-driven marketing

**Why it works:** Emotional trigger → rational solution. Creates urgency. Simple and direct.

**Example:**
> **P:** "Your data team spends 60% of their time answering 'Where is this data?' questions."
> **A:** "That's 3 days a week. $400K/year in engineering time wasted. Meanwhile, business teams make decisions on stale data because they can't find what they need. You're bleeding revenue and talent."
> **S:** "Atlan's active metadata platform surfaces the right data in 10 seconds. Teams find what they need. Engineers build what matters."

**When to avoid:** When the problem isn't obvious or agreed upon. When you're speaking to skeptics who don't feel the pain.

---

## 4. BAB (Before-After-Bridge)

**Structure:**
- **Before**: Life with the problem
- **After**: Life with the solution
- **Bridge**: How to get from Before to After

**Best for:**
- Product demos
- Transformation stories
- Landing pages
- Webinar intros
- Customer testimonials

**Why it works:** Visual contrast. Easy to follow. Focuses on outcome, then explains the path.

**Example:**
> **Before:** "Every week, I spent 4 hours compiling metrics for our exec dashboard. Data was scattered across 7 tools. Half the time, numbers didn't match."
> **After:** "Now the dashboard updates in real-time. I spend 15 minutes reviewing it, not building it. Execs trust the data because lineage is visible."
> **Bridge:** "We implemented Atlan's metadata fabric. It connected our sources, automated lineage, and gave us one source of truth."

**When to avoid:** Complex B2B sales with long evaluation cycles. Situations requiring deep technical credibility.

## 5. Andy Raskin's Strategic Narrative

**Structure:**
1. **Name the Big Change**: What shift is happening in the world?
2. **Show Stakes**: Winners vs. losers in this new world
3. **Tease the Promised Land**: What does winning look like?
4. **Introduce Obstacles**: Why is it hard to get there?
5. **Position Your Solution**: As the bridge to the promised land

**Best for:**
- Series A+ fundraising
- Market category creation
- Strategic positioning
- Thought leadership keynotes
- Competitive differentiation

**Why it works:** Positions you as inevitable, not optional. Makes the market shift the hero, not your product. Creates urgency through external forces.

**Example:**
> 1. "AI is shifting from model training to production deployment. Every company is racing to ship AI products."
> 2. "Winners will ship 10x faster. Losers will spend 18 months on infrastructure and miss the window."
> 3. "Imagine shipping a new AI feature in days, not quarters. Full lineage, governance baked in, no compliance fire drills."
> 4. "But LLMs are black boxes. You can't trace where answers come from. Regulators are circling. One bad output tanks trust."
> 5. "Atlan's AI Observability platform gives you full lineage from prompt to output. Ship fast, stay compliant."

**When to avoid:** Tactical product updates. Incremental improvements. Situations where you need data first, story later.

## 6. Pixar Pitch (Story Spine)

**Structure:**
- Once upon a time, ___
- Every day, ___
- One day, ___
- Because of that, ___
- Because of that, ___
- Until finally, ___
- And ever since then, ___

**Best for:**
- Concept pitches
- Brainstorming sessions
- Quick storytelling exercises
- Explaining ideas to non-experts
- Internal alignment

**Why it works:** Forces you to articulate cause-and-effect. Natural rhythm. Easy to remember and retell.

**Example:**
> "Once upon a time, data teams manually documented every table. Every day, docs got out of date. One day, we automated metadata with AI. Because of that, docs stayed fresh. Because of that, analysts trusted the data. Until finally, the whole company used data confidently. And ever since then, we've scaled metadata without scaling the team."

**When to avoid:** Formal presentations. Anything that requires gravitas. Skeptical audiences who'd see it as childish.

## 7. AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

**Structure:**
- **Attention**: Hook with a bold claim or question
- **Interest**: Build curiosity with details
- **Desire**: Show what they'll gain
- **Action**: Tell them what to do next

**Best for:**
- Email sequences
- Landing pages
- Sales presentations
- Webinar scripts
- Ad campaigns

**Why it works:** Mirrors the buyer journey. Moves from awareness to conversion systematically.

**Example:**
> **A:** "What if your data catalog updated itself?"
> **I:** "Atlan uses AI to detect schema changes, auto-generate docs, and flag breaking changes before they hit production."
> **D:** "Your team stops firefighting. Data engineers build features. Analysts get answers in seconds, not days."
> **A:** "Book a demo to see it live in your stack."

**When to avoid:** Early-stage relationship building. Thought leadership. Situations where immediate action isn't the goal.

## 8. Golden Circle (Simon Sinek)

**Structure:**
- **Why**: Your purpose, cause, belief
- **How**: Your unique process/approach
- **What**: Your products/services

**Best for:**
- Mission statements
- Recruitment pitches
- Cultural talks
- Brand positioning
- Inspirational keynotes

**Why it works:** People don't buy *what* you do, they buy *why* you do it. Starts with emotion, ends with logic.

**Example:**
> **Why:** "We believe data should empower everyone, not just specialists. Knowledge shouldn't be hoarded—it should flow."
> **How:** "We build active metadata—AI that understands context, not just schemas. It brings data to people where they work."
> **What:** "Atlan is a metadata platform for modern data teams."

**When to avoid:** Product demos. Price negotiations. Situations requiring immediate ROI justification.

---

## 9. SCQA (Situation-Complication-Question-Answer)

**Structure:**
- **Situation**: Establish context (neutral)
- **Complication**: Introduce the problem
- **Question**: Pose the key question
- **Answer**: Your solution/recommendation

**Best for:**
- Executive briefs
- Consulting presentations
- Strategic memos
- Board decks
- Business cases

**Why it works:** Rigorous, analytical. Separates facts from problems. Forces clarity on the core question.

**Example:**
> **S:** "We have 47 data sources feeding our analytics warehouse. 12 teams use this data for decision-making."
> **C:** "But 30% of decisions are based on data that's >7 days stale. Teams don't know which tables are fresh vs. deprecated."
> **Q:** "How do we ensure decision-makers use reliable, current data?"
> **A:** "Implement automated freshness SLAs + metadata tagging in Atlan. Teams see data age at query time."

**When to avoid:** Storytelling to non-technical audiences. Emotional or inspirational contexts.

## 10. Nancy Duarte's "What Is / What Could Be"

**Structure:**
- Alternate between **What Is** (current reality) and **What Could Be** (better future)
- Create tension through contrast
- Build to a climax: **The New Bliss**
- Close with a **Call to Adventure**

**Best for:**
- Transformational keynotes
- Vision casting
- Change management talks
- Big product launches
- Cultural shifts

**Why it works:** Creates emotional pendulum. Contrast generates energy. Audience feels the gap viscerally.

**Example:**
> "Today, data is siloed. [What Is] But imagine a world where every question has an instant answer. [What Could Be] Right now, teams wait days for data. [What Is] But they could be making decisions in real-time. [What Could Be] We're stuck in the old way. [What Is] But the future is already here. [New Bliss] Join us in building it. [Call]"

**When to avoid:** Short presentations. Analytical audiences. Situations requiring tactical next steps.

## 11. Minto Pyramid Principle

**Structure:**
- **Answer First**: Lead with the recommendation
- **Supporting Arguments**: Group by 3-5 key reasons
- **Evidence**: Back each reason with data

**Best for:**
- Executive summaries
- Consulting reports
- Strategic recommendations
- Policy memos
- Research papers

**Why it works:** Respects executive time. Lets readers drill down if interested. Logical and scannable.

**Example:**
```
We should implement Atlan's data catalog (Answer)
├── 1. Reduces time-to-insight by 60% (Supporting)
│   ├── Current avg: 4 hours/query
│   └── Atlan users: 1.5 hours/query
├── 2. Prevents $2M in duplicate work (Supporting)
│   └── 3 teams built overlapping pipelines last quarter
└── 3. De-risks compliance (Supporting)
    └── 40% of data has unclear ownership
```

**When to avoid:** Storytelling contexts. Emotional persuasion. Brand building.

## 12. Three-Act Structure

**Structure:**
- **Act I (Setup)**: Establish world, introduce protagonist, inciting incident
- **Act II (Confrontation)**: Rising action, obstacles, midpoint twist, crisis
- **Act III (Resolution)**: Climax, falling action, denouement

**Best for:**
- Case studies
- Customer stories
- Documentary-style content
- Long-form blog posts
- Founder origin stories

**Why it works:** Classic narrative arc. Sustained tension. Emotional payoff.

**Example:**
> **Act I:** "DataCorp had 500 analysts but couldn't answer basic questions. Their VP of Analytics, Maria, was hired to fix it."
> **Act II:** "She tried everything—bought tools, hired consultants, ran training. Nothing stuck. The CEO threatened to cut the team. Maria was out of options."
> **Act III:** "Then she discovered active metadata. Within 3 months, analyst productivity doubled. CEO made her CDO. Today, DataCorp is the most data-driven company in their industry."

**When to avoid:** Short presentations. Time-constrained formats. Analytical contexts.

## When to Use Which: Decision Tree

```
START HERE: What's your goal?

├─ Convince someone to hire you / prove your value
│  └─ STAR
│
├─ Sell a product / generate leads
│  ├─ Cold outreach → PAS
│  ├─ Landing page → BAB or AIDA
│  ├─ Demo → BAB
│  └─ Complex sale → Andy Raskin
│
├─ Inspire / create movement
│  ├─ Internal team → Golden Circle
│  ├─ Big stage keynote → Nancy Duarte
│  └─ Brand story → Hero's Journey
│
├─ Explain a strategy / get approval
│  ├─ To executives → SCQA or Minto Pyramid
│  ├─ To board → Andy Raskin or Minto Pyramid
│  └─ To peers → STAR or Three-Act
│
├─ Pitch an idea quickly
│  └─ Pixar Pitch
│
└─ Document a transformation
   ├─ Customer case study → Hero's Journey or Three-Act
   └─ Internal win → STAR or BAB
```

## Advanced: Combining Frameworks

**The most powerful stories layer frameworks:**

### Example: Customer Case Study (Hero's Journey + STAR)
- **Macro structure**: Hero's Journey for emotional arc
- **Micro structure**: STAR for each key moment
  - Situation = Ordinary World
  - Task = Call to Adventure
  - Action = Tests & Ordeal
  - Result = Reward & Return

### Example: Sales Deck (Andy Raskin + SCQA)
- **Slides 1-5**: Andy Raskin (big change, stakes, promised land)
- **Slides 6-10**: SCQA (your specific situation, complication, question, answer)

### Example: Product Launch (Nancy Duarte + PAS)
- **Opening**: Nancy Duarte (What Is / What Could Be) for vision
- **Middle**: PAS for product positioning
- **Close**: Golden Circle for the mission

## Meta-Framework: The Universal Story Elements

All frameworks share these elements (just in different order):

1. **Status Quo**: What is normal?
2. **Disruption**: What changed or what problem emerged?
3. **Stakes**: Why does this matter?
4. **Struggle**: What obstacles exist?
5. **Resolution**: How is it solved?
6. **Transformation**: What's different now?
7. **Proof**: Why should I believe you?

**The best framework is the one that puts these elements in the order your audience needs to hear them.**

## Pro Tips

1. **Match the framework to the medium**:
   - Email: PAS or AIDA (short, punchy)
   - Slide deck: SCQA or Minto Pyramid (scannable)
   - Keynote: Hero's Journey or Nancy Duarte (emotional)
   - Case study: STAR or Three-Act (detailed)

2. **Start with the end**: Know your call-to-action, then pick the framework that naturally leads there.

3. **Test and iterate**: The "best" framework is the one that resonates with *your* audience. Run A/B tests.

4. **Don't force it**: If a framework feels awkward, try another. Natural storytelling > rigid adherence.

5. **Layer frameworks**: Use a macro framework for structure, micro frameworks for moments.
